# The birth of Romanticism

The **Industrial Revolution** brought about immense changes to society and the way the world conceived work, it was a time of **dramatic changes** in every aspect of life, down to politics, economics, science, labor and, the subject of our study, arts and literature. 

On the morning of **21st January 1793**, in the square of Paris, was erected a contraption simply known as ***The Machine***, proposed by ***Joseph Guillottin***.  At about 10 o'clock that morning, a man lowered his head to the level of a wooden support. The executioner released the blade, which lodged itself into the man's neck. The blade was released a second time; this time, the man's head detatched from his body and fell into a basket. That man was **Louis XVI** the king of France.

It all began **40 years before**, in a world controlled by the elements of hierarchy and authority, there were **no options of climbing the social ladder**. Just the nobles had dignity, money, liberty, and the clergy that had a decent amount of freedom. The other people had no rights, only duties.

In Paris, **police officers were ubiqutous**, about 1 in 545 men worked in the police force. The *"undesired"* people would just disappear. In 1742 two men sit at a café to play chess. They shared their ideas, that became the seeds of Romanticism many years later. These men were ***Denis Diderot* and *Jean-Jacques Rosseau***, they had different ideas but were united by their disliking of the system. Diderot thought the future would be founded on reason, whereas Rousseau belieed in the power of feelings. 

On 24th July 1749 Diderot was woken up by the police knocking on the door of his house. Diderot's crime was that he was thinking differently, he was arrested and locked up in the Bastille. He was writing a monumental book, The *Encyclopedie*, covering the completeness of knowledge, excluding the invertion and existence of God in reality, therefore undermining the authority of the king, given to the monarch by God, replaced by the power of science and civilization. He was accused of heresy and high treason.

Rousseau believed that the key to prison lied in his deep feelings. He believed that man was naturally good, not corrupted by society. He was exiled from France.

America was home to religious radicals and political refugees. Thomas Payne arrived in America after the death of his wife and after bankruptcy. The people in America revolted because of the taxes. Payne wrote a pamphlet called ***Common Sense*** which led to the Declaration of Indipendence in 1776. At the forefront of the Romantic Revolution was the londoner William Blake. In 1779 at the age of 21, William Blake was being educated by Joshua Reynolds, the greatest artist of his time. He explained to Blake the principles of order (societal, economical), unity, harmony, balance and rationality. Blake believed that immaginatio was the force that made great art. He was a visionary artist who poured his ideas into poetrey, drawing and engravings. He and his family lived a life of financial struggle because he had very few readers so he was obliged to publish his works at his own expense.
